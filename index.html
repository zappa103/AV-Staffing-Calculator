How the math works

Weighted BRE = (Active Learning rooms × 3.5) + (all other rooms × 1.9)

This calculator is a condensed version of the full room-by-room complexity audit. In that audit, every AV room on campus is scored on five factors (display, control and switching, audio, video conferencing / lecture capture, and room type), placed into one of four complexity tiers, and weighted accordingly. Summing those weights gives the portfolio's total workload in basic room equivalents.

Analysis of the 2026 audit showed that one question, is this an Active Learning room?, accounts for most of the difference in workload between rooms. Active Learning rooms averaged 3.5 BRE each; all other rooms averaged 1.9 BRE. Those two averages are held constant across every portfolio so that change is measured against a fixed yardstick.

Technicians needed = Weighted BRE ÷ BRE per technician. Gap = technicians needed − technicians on staff. See for terms and the tier weights.

The terms used in this calculator and the complexity audit behind it.

Basic Room Equivalent BRE

The unit of AV support workload. One BRE is the support effort for a single basic, single-display classroom (Tier 1). More complex rooms count as more than one BRE.

Weighted BRE

The total workload for an area or the whole campus: every room's BRE weight added together. Unlike a simple room count, it rises when existing rooms are upgraded with more technology, not only when new rooms are added.

Active Learning room

A room built for group work, with multiple collaboration displays around the walls rather than a single screen at the front, typically with more sources, switching, and audio to match. In the audit these are identified by the wall-display collaboration flag. They are the most support-intensive room type, averaging 3.5 BRE.

Other AV rooms

Every AV-equipped room that is not an Active Learning room, from basic classrooms to large lecture halls with lecture capture. Across the 2026 inventory these averaged 1.9 BRE.

Area

A geographic support zone that a technician or group of technicians covers. The current areas are Central, MLC, North, SLC, and South. Areas can be added or renamed when modeling a proposal.

Seats

Total seating capacity in an area's AV rooms. Shown for reference only; seat count does not drive the staffing estimate, because a large lecture hall is not necessarily harder to support than a small, complex room.

BRE per technician capacity

How much weighted workload one technician can reasonably support, including preventive maintenance, trouble calls, event support, and upgrades. The calculator offers 30, 40, 50, and 60 so the estimate can be viewed across a range of assumptions rather than a single number.

Technicians needed

Weighted BRE divided by BRE per technician: the full-time technician headcount the workload calls for at the selected capacity.

Staffing gap

Technicians needed minus technicians on staff. A positive number (shown in red) means the workload calls for more technicians than are currently on staff; a negative number (green) means current staff exceeds the estimate.

Portfolio

A complete set of rooms-by-area numbers plus the technician headcount. The built-in portfolios are the actual 2020 and 2026 inventories; saved portfolios model proposed changes such as new buildings or renovations.

Scoring factors

Every room is scored on five factors.

  1. Display: number and type of displays and projectors
  2. Control & switching: control system and signal routing
  3. Audio: program audio, voice reinforcement, microphones
  4. Video conferencing / lecture capture: cameras, capture, document cameras
  5. Room type: standard classroom through Active Learning and SCALE-UP

Where the calculator came from

The full audit is the detailed model: every room scored, tiered, and weighted. The calculator compresses it into two inputs per area so the staffing picture can be explored live in a meeting. Using the 2026 audit, the average BRE weight was computed separately for Active Learning rooms (3.5) and all other rooms (1.9). Multiplying each area's room counts by those averages closely reproduces the audit's campus-wide total.

Because the two averages are fixed, the calculator responds to changes in the number and mix of rooms. If the rubric or the typical room build changes significantly, the averages should be recalculated from a fresh audit.
